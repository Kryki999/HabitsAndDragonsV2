## MASTER STYLE LOCK

Use the attached **wizard tavern Style Bible image as the ONLY visual drawing language and absolute ground truth**.

The goal is NOT merely to create a similar cartoon-fantasy image.

The goal is:

**THE EXACT SAME ARTIST, using the exact same drawing habits, is now drawing a new scene for the exact same RPG game.**

Preserve the new scene's requested content and composition, but derive ALL visual execution exclusively from the wizard tavern Style Bible.

---

## 1. LINE ART — CRITICAL

Match the reference's line language precisely.

Use:

* bold,
* clean,
* crisp,
* black or near-black comic outlines,
* visibly stronger exterior outlines,
* slightly thinner internal detail lines.

The outline must remain clearly visible even when the image is viewed small on a phone.

DO NOT use:

* soft brown outlines,
* painterly edges,
* thin illustration linework,
* sketch lines,
* faded outlines,
* realistic edge rendering.

The line art itself must make the image feel like the wizard tavern reference.

---

## 2. SHAPE LANGUAGE

Use the same rounded, chunky, slightly exaggerated fantasy shapes as the Style Bible.

Objects should feel hand-designed for a 2D RPG.

Favor:

* strong silhouettes,
* rounded forms,
* slightly uneven handmade geometry,
* oversized readable props,
* graphic shape design.

Avoid mathematically perfect or photorealistic forms.

---

## 3. CHARACTER DESIGN

Draw all humans, elves, dwarves, goblins and fantasy races using the same facial construction and body language as the Style Bible.

Use:

* expressive cartoon faces,
* slightly exaggerated noses, ears, beards and hands,
* compact readable silhouettes,
* characterful rather than beautiful faces.

Faces should look like game characters from this exact universe.

---

## 4. EYES — CRITICAL

Use:

* simple graphic cartoon eyes,
* restrained pupils,
* simple lids,
* half-lidded eyes for neutral, bored, confident or relaxed expressions when appropriate.

DO NOT use:

* Disney eyes,
* sparkle eyes,
* large cute eyes,
* kawaii eyes,
* glossy oversized pupils,
* generically beautiful female eyes,
* overly charming children's-book expressions.

**Character > prettiness.**

---

## 5. CEL-SHADE — CRITICAL

Render surfaces primarily using:

1. base color,
2. one clear shadow shape,
3. one controlled highlight when needed.

Keep the shading graphic and readable.

Do NOT turn the scene into a painterly or rendered illustration.

Avoid:

* airbrush shading,
* heavy soft gradients,
* realistic skin rendering,
* cinematic physically based lighting,
* excessive specular effects.

---

## 6. SHAPE OVER TEXTURE — CRITICAL

Material identity must come primarily from:

* silhouette,
* color,
* outline,
* large shadow shapes.

Use VERY LIMITED surface texture.

### WOOD

Only a few stylized grain lines.

### STONE

Only a few cracks or block divisions.

### COBBLESTONES

Large simple stones. Minimal internal marks.

### PLASTER

Mostly clean surfaces with occasional stylized wear.

### CLOTH

Large folds, not many small wrinkles.

### METAL

One or two strong graphic highlights.

### GLASS

Simple reflection shapes.

### FOOD / FRUIT / VEGETABLES

Simple shapes and minimal shine.

### WATER

Simple teal/blue shapes with a small number of graphic reflections.

---

## 7. DETAIL BUDGET

The environment may contain many objects, but every individual object must remain graphically simple.

Do NOT solve richness by adding micro-texture.

Reduce:

* excessive wood grain,
* excessive masonry lines,
* excessive roof tile marks,
* excessive cobblestone cracks,
* excessive plaster damage,
* excessive glossy reflections,
* excessive foliage detail.

When uncertain, remove detail.

**Game readability is more important than illustration richness.**

---

## 8. COLOR

Match the Style Bible's saturated but controlled palette.

Use:

* warm browns,
* amber,
* ochre,
* cream,
* terracotta,
* natural green,
* controlled red,
* gold accents.

Fantasy contrast accents may use:

* cyan,
* teal,
* blue,
* purple,
* green.

Do not make every surface equally saturated.

---

## 9. LIGHTING

Use clear stylized game lighting.

Indoor scenes:

* warm candle / fireplace amber.

Outdoor scenes:

* warm daylight or golden-hour sun,
* clean readable shadow groups.

Magic:

* strong controlled cyan/blue/purple/green glow.

Avoid photorealistic cinematic light transport.

---

## 10. ENVIRONMENT DESIGN

Buildings, streets, furniture, vegetation and props must use the SAME cartoon drawing language as the characters.

Architecture should not suddenly become realistic concept art.

Maintain:

* strong outlines,
* simplified materials,
* slightly exaggerated architecture,
* readable building silhouettes.

---

## 11. DEPTH

Decrease detail with distance.

Foreground:

* strongest shapes,
* strongest outlines,
* highest contrast.

Midground:

* moderate detail.

Background:

* simplified shapes,
* reduced contrast,
* less internal linework.

Do not render distant buildings with the same micro-detail as foreground props.

---

## 12. MOBILE GAME LOCATION LOGIC

Treat the image as a **premium 2D mobile RPG location screen**, not a poster or children's-book illustration.

Interactive buildings or important props should be compositionally readable.

Preserve clear:

* focal points,
* foreground / midground / background separation,
* tappable-looking locations,
* visual hierarchy.

---

## 13. MOOD

The world is:

* colorful,
* inviting,
* slightly comedic,
* characterful,
* medieval fantasy RPG.

It is NOT:

* grimdark,
* childish,
* cute children's-book fantasy,
* Disney,
* glossy mobile advertising art.

---

# NEGATIVE STYLE LOCK

Absolutely avoid:

* photorealism
* painterly fantasy art
* realistic concept art
* Disney/Pixar facial design
* sparkle eyes
* kawaii eyes
* thin brown outlines
* soft illustration edges
* excessive textures
* dense wood grain
* dense stone cracks
* over-detailed cobblestones
* realistic glossy fruit
* overly complex water reflections
* realistic skin shading
* cinematic PBR materials
* AI-polished storybook look
* generic fantasy illustration style
* anime aesthetics
* 3D-render appearance

---

# STYLE PRIORITY ORDER

When generating the image, follow this hierarchy:

**1. LINE**
→ **2. SHAPES**
→ **3. FACES / EYES**
→ **4. CEL-SHADE**
→ **5. MATERIAL SIMPLIFICATION**
→ **6. DETAIL BUDGET**
→ **7. COLOR**
→ **8. LIGHTING**
→ **9. GAME-ART READABILITY**

If additional rendering polish conflicts with any higher priority rule, REMOVE THE EXTRA POLISH.

so they obey the wizard tavern Style Bible.

# FINAL SELF-CHECK

Before finalizing, ask:

1. Are the outer outlines as bold and black as the wizard tavern?
2. Do the eyes use the same restrained cartoon language?
3. Are characters characterful rather than generically pretty?
4. Are wood, stone, fabric and food simplified enough?
5. Is the image using cel-shade rather than painterly rendering?
6. Is there too much micro-detail?
7. Does the environment feel like a game location rather than a storybook illustration?
8. At thumbnail size, does it immediately feel like the exact same game?

If ANY answer is no, correct the image before finalizing.
